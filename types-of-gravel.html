Types of Gravel & Sizes Explained

Before you run the numbers through the pea gravel calculator, it helps to know what you're actually ordering. Here's a quick reference for the most common gravel types, their sizes, and what each is best used for.

TypeTypical sizeDensityBest for
Pea gravel3/8"–1/2"~1.4 tons/yd³Walkways, patios, playgrounds, ground cover
River rock1"–4"+~1.5 tons/yd³Drainage beds, borders, dry creek beds
Crushed stone3/8"–1.5"~1.5 tons/yd³Driveway base, under patios, drainage

Decorative/landscape gravel1/4"–1"~1.4 tons/yd³Garden beds, mulch alternative, accents

Pea gravel vs. river rock

Pea gravel is smaller — 3/8 to 1/2 inch — smooth, rounded, and naturally multicolored. It's comfortable to walk on and works well for pathways, patios, and play areas. River rock starts around 1 inch and goes up to several inches; it's better suited to drainage beds, dry creek features, and larger decorative borders where you want fewer, bigger stones.

Not sure which you need? Run your numbers through the pea gravel calculator or the river rock calculator — both use the same fast method, just different densities and typical depths.

Frequently asked questions

What size is pea gravel?

Pea gravel is typically 3/8 inch to 1/2 inch in diameter — roughly the size of a pea, which is where it gets its name. It's naturally rounded and smooth from water erosion.

What's the difference between pea gravel and river rock?

Pea gravel is smaller (3/8–1/2 inch) and used for pathways, patios and playgrounds. River rock is larger (1 inch and up) and used for drainage beds, borders and bigger decorative features.

What is crushed stone used for?

Crushed stone has angular, machine-broken edges that interlock when compacted, making it the standard choice for driveway bases, under patios, and drainage applications.
