Types of Gravel: Complete Guide to Sizes, Uses & Cost

The main types of gravel are pea gravel, crushed stone such as #57, crusher run, river rock, decomposed granite and bank run. Choose angular crushed stone when you need a firm, interlocking base for a driveway or patio, and rounded gravel when you want drainage or a decorative finish. The table below compares 15 common types by size, weight and best use.

Gravel vs crushed stone vs rock: what is the difference?

Gravel is naturally weathered stone, usually rounded from water or ice. Crushed stone is quarried rock broken into angular pieces and screened to size. Rock or rip rap describes large stone, usually over 6 inches.

Angular vs rounded: the 10-second rule

If the stone will carry weight, such as a driveway, shed base or patio base, use angular stone, because the sharp faces lock together. If the stone is for looks, play or drainage, rounded stone is fine, but it will shift underfoot and under tires.

Washed vs unwashed gravel

Washed stone has the dust and fines removed, so water flows through it and it stays loose. Unwashed stone keeps its fines, which helps it pack down firm. Use washed stone for drainage and unwashed (with fines) for bases and driveway surfaces.

Pea gravel

Small, smooth, rounded stone, comfortable underfoot and an easy way to cover paths, patios and play areas over a compacted base with edging. It drains well but shifts, so it is not a driveway surface. Use the pea gravel calculator to work out tons, yards and bags for your area.

Crushed stone: #57, #3, #8 and #411

Crushed stone is sold by ASTM D448 size numbers. #57 (about 3/4 to 1 inch, clean) is the all-round base and drainage stone. #3 (about 1 to 2 inches) is for deep subbases and trench drains. #8 and #89 (about 3/8 inch and finer) are for pipe bedding and fine drainage. #411 is about 3/4 inch with fines and compacts into a firm surface.

What does #57 stone mean?

The number comes from the ASTM D448 sizing table. #57 stone is a mix of pieces from about 1 inch down to roughly 3/16 inch, with little fines, which is why it drains well and locks together.

Crusher run (DGA)

Crusher run, also called dense-graded aggregate, is stone crushed with its fines left in. It compacts hard, which makes it ideal for subbases, driveway surfaces and paver bases, but it drains less freely than clean stone. See the gravel driveway calculator for how it fits into a full driveway layer build.

River rock

Smooth, rounded stone from about 1 to 5 inches, used for decorative beds, tree rings, dry creek beds and drainage. Heavy, long-lasting and more expensive than structural stone. Use the river rock calculator for size-specific coverage and depth guidance.

Decomposed granite

Fine, crushed granite that packs into a firm, natural-looking surface for paths and patios. It compacts more than gravel and needs edging, and its fines can track indoors.

Limestone gravel

Crushed limestone is a common, relatively low-cost driveway and base stone. It is angular, compacts well and is widely available, though it can raise the alkalinity of runoff, which matters near plantings that need acid soil.

Marble chips and lava rock

Marble chips are bright, angular decorative stone for beds and borders. Lava rock is lightweight and porous, used for decorative cover; check the weight before calculating, because it is far lighter than gravel.

Bank run, rip rap, crushed concrete and screenings

  • Bank run is mixed sand and gravel dug from a pit; it is cheap fill and rough base.
  • Rip rap is large angular stone used to stop erosion along banks, culverts and shorelines.
  • Crushed concrete (recycled) is a low-cost, compactable base made from broken slabs.
  • Screenings (stone dust) are the finest crushed stone, used for leveling under pavers and for path surfaces.

Frequently asked questions

What size is pea gravel?

Pea gravel is typically about 1/8 to 3/8 inch in diameter, smooth and rounded by water rather than crushed.

What is the difference between pea gravel and river rock?

Pea gravel is small and rounded, at about 1/8 to 3/8 inch. River rock is much larger, usually 1 to 5 inches, though both share the same naturally rounded shape.

What is crushed stone used for?

Crushed stone is used as a base for driveways, patios and shed pads, for drainage beds and French drains, and as an aggregate mixed into concrete.

What is the difference between crusher run and crushed stone?

Crusher run keeps its fines (stone dust) mixed in, so it compacts into a firm, low-draining surface. Clean crushed stone, like #57, has the fines screened out, so it drains freely instead.

Is limestone gravel good for a driveway?

Yes. Crushed limestone is a common, relatively affordable driveway and base stone that compacts well, though it's softer than granite-based stone and can wear faster under heavy traffic.

What is the cheapest type of gravel?

Bank run, crushed concrete and crusher run are usually the least expensive, since they need the least processing and are often sourced locally.

What kind of gravel hardens like concrete?

Crusher run or #411, both of which keep their fines. Once compacted and wetted, they pack into a firm, almost solid layer, though neither actually cures like concrete.

What gravel drains best?

Clean, washed stone with no fines, such as washed #57 or #3, drains best because there's no dust to fill the gaps between stones.

Method and sources

Size designations follow ASTM D448; densities are typical planning figures, and both vary by quarry and region, so confirm specifics with your supplier before ordering.
