How much gravel do I need?

Multiply length by width by depth, then convert to cubic yards. With depth in inches:

cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 324

Then multiply by the material's density to get tons. Add 10% for compaction and waste.

Gravel tonnage calculator: from yards to tons

Quarries sell by the ton, so the tonnage is the number to order. Multiply cubic yards by about 1.4 tons per cubic yard. This yard of gravel calculator does both steps for you: enter the area above and read the cubic yards and the tons together. If you already know the area, the gravel square foot calculator starts from square feet.

Worked examples

  • Garden bed, 4 x 20 ft at 2 inches: 80 sq ft x 2 ÷ 324 = 0.49 cubic yards, 0.69 tons. Order 0.54 cubic yards (0.76 tons) with 10% extra.
  • Patio base, 10 x 12 ft at 3 inches: 120 sq ft x 3 ÷ 324 = 1.11 cubic yards, 1.56 tons. Order 1.22 cubic yards (1.7 tons).
  • Driveway, 12 x 50 ft at 4 inches: 600 sq ft x 4 ÷ 324 = 7.41 cubic yards, 10.4 tons. Order 8.2 cubic yards (11.4 tons).

How many tons are in a cubic yard of gravel?

About 1.4 tons, or 2,800 lb, for common gravel and crushed stone, though it ranges from about 1.3 to 1.6 tons depending on the stone, moisture and size. Gravel is sold by the ton at most quarries and by the cubic yard at many landscape yards, so you often need to convert.

How many yards are in a ton of gravel?

About 0.71 cubic yards at 1.4 tons per cubic yard (1 divided by 1.4). To convert, divide tons by the density or multiply cubic yards by it.

How much is 1, 2, 5 or 6 tons of gravel?

One ton of gravel is about 0.71 cubic yards (at 1.4 tons per cubic yard), enough to cover about 77 square feet at 3 inches deep. The price depends on your supplier: multiply the tons by their price per ton, or the cubic yards by their price per yard. One cubic yard of gravel is 27 cubic feet and weighs about 1.4 tons.

cost = tons × price per ton  or  cost = cubic yards × price per cubic yard

Convert square yards to tons

A square yard is 9 square feet. Tons per square yard depend on the depth: tons = depth in inches ÷ 36 × 1.4. For example, 1 square yard at 3 inches deep holds about 0.16 tons.

How much extra gravel should I order for compaction and waste?

Add 10% to the calculated volume on firm, level ground, and 15% on soft ground, over a deep base, or when you are filling a trench. Crushed stone with fines compacts more than clean rounded stone, and some material is always lost to the edges and the spreading. Order to the next half cubic yard, because a second delivery costs more than a few extra shovels of stone.

How much does a truckload of gravel hold?

A tandem-axle dump truck commonly carries about 10 to 15 tons, which is roughly 7 to 11 cubic yards of gravel, but confirm the capacity with your supplier. A pickup bed can hold the volume of a cubic yard or more, yet a cubic yard of gravel weighs about 2,800 lb, which is more than many half-ton pickups can legally carry, so weight, not volume, is the limit. One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, or about 4.5 loads in a standard 6 cubic foot wheelbarrow.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate how much gravel I need?

Multiply length x width x depth in feet to get cubic feet, divide by 27 for cubic yards, and multiply by about 1.4 for tons. Add 10%.

How much does a cubic yard of gravel cost?

It depends on the stone, region and delivery distance. Get local quotes, then multiply your cubic yards by the quoted price using the Gravel Cost Calculator.

What is the difference between gravel, crushed stone and sand?

Gravel is naturally rounded stone, crushed stone is broken rock with angular faces, and sand is much finer. Angular stone locks together for bases; rounded stone drains and looks natural.

How do I measure an irregular area?

Split it into rectangles, circles and triangles, calculate each area, and add them.

Method and sources

Volume = length × width × depth, converted to cubic yards (27 cubic feet per cubic yard, or 324 for depth in inches). Weight = volume × density. Stone size numbers follow ASTM D448. Figures are planning estimates; confirm density and quantity with your supplier.
