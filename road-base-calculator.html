How to calculate road base

Treat the finished, compacted layer as the volume you want, then add a compaction allowance. Dense graded base typically compacts by roughly 15 to 25%, depending on the material and how well it is compacted.

cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × compacted depth (in) ÷ 324
order = cubic yards × (1 + compaction %)

Example: a 100 x 12 ft road at 4 inches compacted is 1,200 x 4 ÷ 324 = 14.8 cubic yards. With 20% for compaction, order about 17.8 cubic yards, roughly 26.7 tons at 1.5 tons per cubic yard.

Build thick bases in lifts of 4 to 6 inches and compact each one. For driveways see the gravel driveway calculator, which uses a top layer of gravel and a base.

Road base, base rock, ABC and flex base

Names vary by region. Road base, base rock, ABC (aggregate base course), crusher run and flex base are all crushed stone mixed with fines that packs into a firm layer. They weigh about 1.4 to 1.6 tons per cubic yard. Ask the supplier which product meets your local specification.

How much area does a ton of road base cover?

At 1.5 tons per cubic yard, one ton is about 0.67 cubic yards loose. After compaction it covers roughly 45 to 55 square feet at 4 inches. Use the calculator above for your exact area, because compaction changes the answer.

Road base vs gravel

Road base has fines that lock together under compaction, so it makes a firm layer. Clean gravel does not compact, which is better for drainage and decorative use. See the types of gravel guide for the difference.

Road base calculator FAQ

How much road base do I need?

Multiply length by width in feet and compacted thickness in inches, divide by 324, then add 15 to 25% for compaction.

How much does a cubic yard of road base weigh?

About 1.4 to 1.6 tons, so plan on about 1.5 tons.

How thick should road base be?

Often 4 to 6 inches for a driveway, more over soft ground. Check your local specification.

Why add extra for compaction?

Road base loose volume shrinks as it is compacted, so you need more than the finished volume.

Is this calculator exact?

No. It is a planning estimate. Confirm the density and quantity with your supplier.

How much road base do I need for a driveway 200 feet long, 10 feet wide and 6 inches deep?

About 37.04 cubic yards, or roughly 55.6 tons at 1.5 tons per cubic yard. With 20% extra, order about 44.44 cubic yards or 66.7 tons.

Why does this page start with 20% extra?

Base is typically spread and compacted in layers, and long narrow jobs lose material at the edges and over soft spots, so this page starts at 20% instead of the 10% used on the other calculators. Change the extra field if your supplier or contractor recommends a different allowance.
