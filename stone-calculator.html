How to calculate tons of stone

Find the volume first, then multiply by the weight of the stone. Stone is sold by the ton, so the volume alone does not tell you what to order.

cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 324
tons = cubic yards × tons per cubic yard

Example: a 20 x 10 ft area, 3 inches deep, is 200 x 3 ÷ 324 = 1.85 cubic yards. At 1.4 tons per cubic yard that is 2.59 tons, or about 2.85 tons with 10% extra.

How many cubic yards are in a ton of stone?

It depends on the stone. A ton of most crushed stone fills roughly 0.65 to 0.75 cubic yards, because a cubic yard weighs about 1.35 to 1.55 tons. Figures below are planning densities; the real value depends on moisture, gradation and the quarry, so ask your supplier for their tons-per-yard figure.

How much does a cubic yard of crushed stone weigh?

Plan on about 1.4 tons (2,800 lb) per cubic yard for clean 3/4 inch crushed stone. Stone with fines, such as limestone screenings, packs tighter and weighs more, up to about 1.6 tons per cubic yard. Rounded river rock and large landscape stone weigh a little less because of the air between the pieces.

Chippings, crushed rock and landscape rock

The same maths works for chippings (the UK word for small crushed stone), crushed rock and landscape rock. Use cubic yards for the volume, then pick the nearest stone in the list for the weight. The river rock calculator has its own page for larger decorative stone, and the gravel calculator covers general gravel.

Stone calculator FAQ

How do I calculate tons of stone?

Multiply length by width in feet and depth in inches, divide by 324 to get cubic yards, then multiply by about 1.4 tons per cubic yard for crushed stone.

How many cubic yards are in a ton of stone?

About 0.65 to 0.75 cubic yards, because crushed stone weighs about 1.35 to 1.55 tons per cubic yard.

How much does a cubic yard of crushed stone weigh?

About 1.4 tons (2,800 lb) for clean 3/4 inch stone. Stone with fines can weigh up to about 1.6 tons.

How much extra stone should I order?

Add about 10% for compaction and waste, and up to 15% over soft ground or when filling a trench.

Is this calculator exact?

No. It is a planning estimate. Confirm the density and the quantity with your supplier before you order.
