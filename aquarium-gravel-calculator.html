How much gravel for a fish tank?

A common rule of thumb is about 1 pound of gravel per gallon of water for a 1 inch layer, and about 1.5 to 2 inches deep is typical for gravel. The calculator above uses the actual tank floor area, which is more accurate than the gallons rule.

cubic feet = length (in) × width (in) × depth (in) ÷ 1,728
pounds = cubic feet × density (lb per cubic foot)

Example: a 48 x 13 inch tank floor with 2 inches of gravel is 1,248 cubic inches, or 0.72 cubic feet. At about 100 lb per cubic foot that is about 72 lb.

Aquarium gravel depth

For gravel, about 1.5 to 2 inches is common. For planted tanks, 2 to 3 inches helps roots. For sand, 1 to 2 inches is common, and thicker sand can trap gas. The right depth depends on your fish and plants.

Bagged gravel

German readers can use the Aquarium Kies Rechner, which works in centimetres and kilograms. Aquarium gravel is usually sold in 5, 10, 20 and 25 lb bags. Round up to the next full bag, and rinse the gravel before use.

Fish tank gravel FAQ

How much gravel do I need for a fish tank?

Multiply tank length, width and substrate depth in inches, divide by 1,728 for cubic feet, then multiply by about 100 pounds per cubic foot.

How deep should aquarium gravel be?

About 1.5 to 2 inches is common for gravel.

How many pounds of gravel for a 20 gallon tank?

A standard 20 gallon long tank (30 x 12 in) with 2 inches of gravel needs about 42 lb.

Is this calculator exact?

No. It is a planning estimate. Check the weight and density on the bag.

How many pounds of gravel do I need for a 20-gallon long tank?

A footprint of about 30 × 12 inches with 2 inches of gravel is about 0.42 cubic feet, or about 42 lb at 100 lb per cubic foot. With 5% extra, buy about 44 lb. Measure your own tank, because dimensions vary by brand.

How do I calculate gravel for a sloped substrate?

Use the average depth. For example, a layer that is 1 inch deep at the front and 4 inches deep at the back averages 2.5 inches, so enter 2.5 in the substrate depth field.
