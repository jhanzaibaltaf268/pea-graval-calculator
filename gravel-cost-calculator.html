How gravel cost is calculated

Cost comes from five steps: find the volume, convert it to weight, add an allowance for compaction and waste, multiply by your price per ton, then add delivery and any spreading or compaction.

1. Volume: length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 324 = cubic yards
2. Weight: cubic yards × density (about 1.4 tons per cubic yard)
3. Allowance: add 10% for compaction and waste
4. Material cost: tons × price per ton
5. Total: material cost + delivery + spreading and compaction

Price per ton vs per cubic yard vs per bag

Suppliers quote in different units, so convert before you compare.

  • Per ton to per cubic yard: price per ton × 1.4.
  • Per cubic yard to per ton: price per cubic yard ÷ 1.4.
  • Per bag to per cubic yard: bag price × 54 for 0.5 cubic foot bags (27 cubic feet per yard ÷ 0.5).

Is it cheaper to buy gravel by the ton or by the yard?

Neither is cheaper by itself; the same stone costs the same once you convert. What matters is the density the supplier uses. A seller who charges by the yard for a heavy stone may be charging more per ton than one who sells by weight, so convert both quotes to price per ton.

Gravel prices by material

Cost depends on how much processing the stone needs and how far it travels. Use this as a guide to relative cost and ask suppliers for current prices.

Decorative stone usually costs the most and base stone the least, so using crushed stone for the base and a thin decorative layer on top is the usual way to control the budget.

Delivery costs by distance

Delivery is often the biggest variable. Suppliers typically charge a flat fee per load or a rate based on distance, and many have a minimum order. Price per ton delivered is the figure to compare. A few miles can change the total noticeably on a small order, so ask for the price delivered to your address, not the pit price.

Does gravel delivery cost extra?

Yes, in most cases. Some yards include a short radius in the price, others charge a fee per load, and small orders under the minimum may carry a surcharge. Always ask whether the quote is picked up or delivered.

How much does a dump truck load of gravel cost?

A typical tandem dump truck holds about 10 to 15 tons. Multiply the truck's tons by your price per ton for the material, then add delivery. For smaller jobs, a smaller truck or a trailer load may be available at a higher rate per ton.

Worked cost estimate

The example below uses the formula with your own price per ton substituted in; it does not assume a specific dollar figure.

A 10 x 20 ft driveway apron, 4 inches deep:

  • Area: 200 sq ft.
  • Volume: 200 × 4 ÷ 324 = 2.47 cubic yards.
  • Weight: 2.47 × 1.4 = 3.46 tons; with 10% allowance, 3.80 tons.
  • Material cost: tons × your price per ton.
  • Cost per square foot: material cost ÷ 200.

The same job costs more once you add spreading, compaction and any fabric or edging. Always budget for those separately.

Bulk vs bags cost comparison

Bags cost more per cubic yard because you pay for packaging and retail handling, but they have no delivery fee. Multiply a bag's price by 54 (for 0.5 cubic foot bags) to get its per-cubic-yard cost, and compare that with a bulk quote plus delivery. The break-even depends on delivery fees and minimums, but bulk usually wins above about half a cubic yard, and bags win for patches, repairs and small beds.

Other costs to include

  • Spreading and grading, by hand or with a skid steer
  • Compaction equipment hire
  • Landscape or geotextile fabric
  • Edging
  • Excavation or removal of existing material
  • Seasonal price changes, since quarries can charge more in peak building season

How to lower your gravel cost

  • Use crushed stone or bank run for depth and keep decorative stone to the top 1 to 2 inches.
  • Compare two or three suppliers priced per ton delivered.
  • Order a whole load when you can; small loads cost more per ton.
  • Measure carefully and add only 10%, since over-ordering is the most common waste.
  • Ask about returns or pickup of leftover stone.

Frequently asked questions

How much does gravel cost per ton?

It varies by stone, region and distance. Get current quotes from local suppliers; the calculator turns any price into a total.

How much does gravel cost per cubic yard?

Price per ton x 1.4. Multiply your supplier's per-ton price by 1.4 to estimate the per-cubic-yard price.

What is the cheapest type of gravel for a driveway?

Usually crusher run, bank run or #57 crushed stone, because they are produced in volume and need little finishing.

What is a delivery minimum?

The smallest order a supplier will deliver, either in tons or as a minimum charge. It makes very small orders cost more per ton.

How much does it cost to have gravel spread?

It depends on the area, access and equipment. Ask contractors for a quote per square foot or per hour; the calculator's cost per square foot helps you compare.

Why is river rock more expensive than crushed stone?

It is selected and washed for appearance and sorted by size and colour, so it costs more to produce than structural stone.

Should I get multiple quotes?

Yes. Prices vary by supplier and distance, and quotes are quick to compare once converted to price per ton delivered.

Method and sources

Cost = tons × price + delivery. Tons = cubic yards × density, with density defaulting to 1.4 tons per cubic yard. This page does not quote specific market prices; enter your own supplier's quote in the calculator above for an accurate total.
