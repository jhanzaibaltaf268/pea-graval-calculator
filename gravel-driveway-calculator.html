How to estimate how much gravel a driveway needs

Find the area, multiply by the depth, and convert to cubic yards. In imperial units:

cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 324

Then convert to tons with the density of your stone, usually 1.3 to 1.5 tons per cubic yard for crushed stone. Repeat for each layer, add the layers together, and add 10% for compaction and waste.

How many cubic yards of gravel do I need?

A driveway 12 ft wide and 50 ft long is 600 square feet. At 4 inches deep, that is 600 × 4 ÷ 324 = 7.41 cubic yards. With a 10% allowance, order 8.15 cubic yards, rounded up to 8.2.

How many tons of gravel do I need?

Multiply cubic yards by the density. At 1.4 tons per cubic yard, 7.41 cubic yards weighs 10.4 tons, or 11.4 tons with the 10% allowance. Ask your supplier which density they use, because tons are how most quarries bill.

Recommended gravel driveway layers

A stable driveway is built from the bottom up, with coarser stone below and finer stone on top. The depths below are typical for residential driveways; heavier traffic and softer soil need more.

Layer 1: Subbase (4 to 6 inches)

The subbase is the structural layer. Use a coarse, well-graded stone such as crusher run or road base, spread in lifts of no more than about 4 inches, and compact each lift. On soft or clay soil, lay geotextile fabric first so the stone does not sink into the subgrade.

Layer 2: Base course (2 to 3 inches)

The base course is usually #57 crushed stone, a clean, angular stone about 3/4 inch across. The angular faces lock together and the gaps let water drain, which keeps the driveway from turning to mud.

Layer 3: Surface course (1 to 2 inches)

The surface layer is a stone with fines, such as #411 or crusher run, that compacts into a firm, even surface. Avoid rounded stone such as pea gravel as the main surface, because it rolls under tires; see the Pea Gravel Calculator if you want it for a decorative top dressing.

What is the best gravel for a driveway base?

A coarse, angular, compactable stone: crusher run for the subbase and #57 for the base course. Angular faces interlock under load; rounded stone does not.

What is the best gravel for a driveway surface?

A stone with fines, such as #411 or crusher run, that packs into a firm top and sheds water. Clean stone without fines stays loose and shifts.

How much gravel for a 100 ft driveway?

A 12 ft wide, 100 ft long driveway at 4 inches needs 14.8 cubic yards, about 20.7 tons, or 22.8 tons with a 10% allowance. For a three-layer build, calculate each layer as in the worked example.

How much extra gravel to order for compaction and waste

Crushed stone compacts by roughly 10 to 15% once it is rolled or plate-compacted, and some is lost at the edges. Add 10% for a firm, level subgrade and 15% for soft ground or a deep subbase. Order in whole truckloads where you can, and keep a few tons back for later top-ups, because driveways lose stone to traffic, plowing and rain over time.

Crown, ditches and drainage

A driveway that holds water will rut, however thick the stone. Build a crown, with the center about 2 to 3 inches higher than the edges per 12 feet of width, or slope the whole surface to one side. Give runoff somewhere to go with a ditch, culvert or grassed swale, and lay geotextile fabric over clay or soft subgrade to stop the stone sinking. Allow for the crown and for any extra depth in low spots in your volume; the calculator's waste allowance covers small variations, not large regrading.

Gravel driveway cost breakdown

Material cost = tons × price per ton, or cubic yards × price per cubic yard. Total project cost also includes:

  • Delivery. Usually charged per load or by distance, with a minimum order.
  • Spreading and grading. Hand labor, a skid steer or a grader.
  • Compaction. Plate compactor or roller hire.
  • Geotextile fabric, edging and drainage. Small cost per square foot, but needed on soft ground.
  • Excavation. If you remove topsoil or regrade first.

Prices vary widely by region, stone type and distance from the quarry, so get at least two local quotes, priced per ton delivered. Common gravel driveway estimating mistakes

  • Ordering only the surface layer. A 2-inch top over bare soil sinks within a year; budget for the subbase and base.
  • Using pea gravel or other rounded stone as the surface. It shifts and rolls; use angular stone with fines.
  • Forgetting compaction. Loose stone settles 10 to 15%; add the allowance.
  • Mixing densities. Crusher run, #57 and pea gravel weigh differently; set the density per layer.
  • Ignoring drainage. Without a crown or ditch, water undermines the base.
  • Skipping fabric on soft soil. Stone mixes into clay and the layers lose their strength.
  • Not checking truck access. A loaded truck needs room to turn and clearance for its weight on your soil.

Frequently asked questions

How much gravel do I need for my driveway?

A 12 x 50 ft driveway at 4 inches needs 7.4 cubic yards (about 10.4 tons), or 8.2 cubic yards (11.4 tons) with a 10% allowance. A three-layer build of the same driveway needs about 16.7 cubic yards (24.2 tons) before the allowance. Enter your own size and depths in the calculator.

How deep should each gravel layer be?

Typically 4 to 6 inches for the subbase, 2 to 3 inches for the base course and 1 to 2 inches for the surface. A light-use driveway on firm soil can often manage with 4 to 6 inches of compacted stone in total.

How many tons of gravel do I need for a driveway?

Multiply cubic yards by the stone's density, usually 1.3 to 1.5 tons per cubic yard. A 12 x 100 ft driveway at 4 inches is about 14.8 cubic yards, or 20.7 tons.

How much does driveway gravel cost?

It depends on your region, the stone, and the distance from the quarry. Get local quotes per ton delivered, enter the price in the calculator, and add spreading, compaction and drainage costs if you are hiring help. Use the Gravel Cost Calculator for a full breakdown.

Do I need more than one layer of gravel?

Usually yes, if the driveway will carry regular traffic or the soil is soft. Layers of different stone give strength below, drainage in the middle and a firm surface on top. On firm, well-drained soil, one compacted layer of crusher run or #411 can be enough for light use.

What is the difference between subbase, base course and surface course?

The subbase is the thick coarse bottom layer that spreads the load. The base course is a clean angular stone such as #57 that drains and interlocks. The surface course is a finer stone with fines that compacts into a smooth top.

How much extra gravel should I order?

Add 10% for compaction and waste on firm ground, or 15% on soft ground or a deep subbase.

Can I use pea gravel for a driveway?

Not as the main surface. Rounded stone rolls under tires and does not lock together. Use angular crushed stone and keep pea gravel for decorative edges; see the Pea Gravel Calculator.

How do I calculate gravel for a driveway that is not a rectangle?

Split it into rectangles, circles and triangles, work out the area of each, and add them together. Curved driveways can be measured by the length along the center line times the average width.

Method and sources

Volume = length × width × depth, converted to cubic yards (27 cubic feet per cubic yard, or 324 when depth is in inches). Weight = volume × density, calculated separately for each layer. Stone size numbers follow the ASTM D448 system. Layer depths and densities are typical planning figures, not engineering specifications; confirm them with your supplier, and ask a local contractor or engineer for a heavy-load or steep-site design.
