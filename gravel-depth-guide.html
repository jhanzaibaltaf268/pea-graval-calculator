Why gravel depth matters

Depth isn't just about how much material you buy — it's what makes gravel actually do its job. Too shallow, and a decorative layer wears thin fast, showing bare landscape fabric or dirt within a season; a walkway base that's too thin shifts and ruts under regular foot traffic; a driveway without enough compacted base flexes under vehicle weight and develops potholes. Too deep wastes money on material that isn't adding function.

Stability

A layer that is too thin does not spread the load, so wheels and feet push the stone into the soil underneath and you end up with ruts and soft spots.

Drainage

Depth gives water somewhere to go. A thicker layer of clean, open-graded stone stores and moves water away from the surface instead of letting it pool.

Appearance

Enough depth hides the soil and landscape fabric underneath. For decorative gravel, 2 inches is usually the minimum that stays covered as the stone settles and shifts over time.

Longevity

A proper depth means fewer top-ups. Thin gravel works its way into the ground and disappears within a season or two, while a correctly built base interacts well with the local frost line, so water doesn't collect and freeze underneath and heave the surface over winter.

Depth details by project type

Driveways: 4 to 6 inches or more

Build in layers: 4 to 6 inches of coarse subbase, 2 to 3 inches of #57 and 1 to 2 inches of surface stone. Heavy vehicles or soft ground need 8 to 12 inches in total. Use the gravel driveway calculator for a layered material breakdown.

Walkways and paths: 2 inches

A path only takes foot traffic, so 2 inches of pea gravel or decomposed granite over a compacted base is enough. Edging keeps the stone from migrating onto the lawn.

Patios and garden seating: 3 to 4 inches

Use 3 to 4 inches of compacted crushed stone as a base and finish with 1 to 2 inches of the surface stone, with edging to hold the line.

French drains: 12 inches or more

Fill the trench with washed stone, wrap it in geotextile fabric and slope the pipe. The exact depth depends on the water volume you need to move and the pipe size.

Shed bases: 4 to 6 inches

Extend the base 6 to 12 inches beyond the shed footprint on every side, and compact each lift before adding the next.

Parking areas: 4 to 6 inches

Treat a parking area like a driveway: use a subbase if vehicles will park there regularly, and more depth if the ground is soft or sees heavy traffic.

Playgrounds

The required depth depends on the fall height of the equipment and the loose-fill material, and your local safety standard governs it. Check the standard that applies before you build.

Dog runs: 2 to 3 inches

Pea gravel or washed stone drains fast and is easy on paws over a compacted base; plan to rinse or top up the stone periodically.

How depth affects how much gravel you need

Because volume is area × depth, doubling your depth doubles your material and your cost — the relationship is directly linear, and it's the single biggest lever in any gravel estimate. A 200 sq ft patio at 2 inches needs about 1.23 cubic yards; the same patio at 4 inches needs about 2.47 cubic yards — roughly double the tons, the delivery cost, and (if you're buying bagged) the number of bags. That's why getting depth right before you order matters more than getting your area measurement precise to the inch.

Loose depth vs compacted depth

The depths in this guide are finished, compacted depths — what the gravel measures once it's settled and, for structural layers, been tamped or rolled down. Freshly spread, uncompacted gravel sits noticeably higher than that, since loose material has more air gaps between stones. Gravel typically settles 10 to 15% when compacted, so if you need 4 inches finished, spread about 4.5 inches loose, or add 10 to 15% to your order. Compact each lift of no more than 4 inches, and keep leftover stone for later top-ups. Purely decorative materials like pea gravel and river rock aren't compacted the same way, so what you spread is close to what you keep.

Factors that affect gravel depth

  • Soil type: clay and soft soil need more depth and fabric to keep the stone from sinking.
  • Traffic load: cars need more depth than feet; trucks need more than cars.
  • Drainage and water table: wet sites need coarser, more open-graded stone and more depth.
  • Climate: freeze-thaw cycles can heave an undersized base over winter.
  • Stone type: angular stone compacts and locks together better than rounded stone, so it needs less depth for the same strength.

Frequently asked questions

How deep should gravel be?

About 2 inches for paths, 3 to 4 inches for patios, and 4 to 6 inches or more for driveways and shed bases. It depends on the use: light foot traffic needs less depth than vehicle traffic or a structural load.

Is 2 inches of gravel enough?

For a path, garden bed or dog run over a compacted base, yes. For a driveway, parking area or shed base, no — those need 4 inches or more of compacted base material.

How deep should gravel be under a shed?

Most sheds want 4–6 inches of compacted crushed stone as a base, which levels the ground and keeps moisture from sitting under the floor. Check your specific shed manufacturer's foundation requirements, since some prefill or kit foundations specify their own depth.

How deep should gravel be for a patio?

3 to 4 inches of compacted crushed stone as a base, finished with 1 to 2 inches of the surface stone, with edging to hold the line.

How deep should gravel be for a French drain?

Fill the trench with washed stone over the pipe, usually 12 inches or more depending on the water volume, wrapped in geotextile fabric.

Does a deeper gravel layer mean better drainage?

Not by itself. Drainage depends more on the material's gradation than its depth — clean, washed stone with a proper slope drains better than a deeper layer of dense-graded material alone.

Should I spread gravel deeper than the target depth?

Slightly, for compactable base materials: about 10 to 15% more loose depth, which settles down to the target once tamped or rolled. Decorative materials like pea gravel aren't compacted, so spread those close to the target depth.

Method and sources

Depths above are typical residential guidance, not engineering specifications. Confirm local codes, frost-line depth and your equipment's requirements before building a driveway, shed base or playground surface, and check your supplier's density figure for converting between volume and weight.
