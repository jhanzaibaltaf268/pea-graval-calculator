Gravel Depth Guide: How Deep Should Gravel Be?

The right depth depends entirely on what the gravel needs to do. Once you know your depth, plug it into the pea gravel calculator along with your area to get exact quantities.

Recommended Depth by Project

Use caseRecommended depthNotes
Decorative ground cover2 inLight foot traffic only
Garden beds & borders2 inOver landscape fabric to suppress weeds
Walkways2–3 inCompact a gravel base first for stability
Patios3 inAdd a compacted base layer underneath
Drainage beds / French drains3–4 inUse larger river rock or crushed stone
Shed base / small outbuilding4–6 inCompacted crushed stone; check the shed maker's spec
Driveways (base layer)4–6 inCrushed stone or road base, compacted
Driveways (decorative top layer)1–2 inApplied over the compacted base

These are general guidelines — check local frost-line and drainage requirements for structural applications like driveways and shed bases.

Why Depth Matters

Depth isn't just about how much material you buy — it's what makes gravel actually do its job. Too shallow, and a decorative layer wears thin fast, showing bare landscape fabric or dirt within a season; a walkway base that's too thin shifts and ruts under regular foot traffic; a driveway without enough compacted base flexes under vehicle weight and develops potholes. Too deep wastes money on material that isn't adding function — a 6-inch decorative layer costs three times what a 2-inch layer does for a look that isn't three times better.

For structural uses like driveways and shed bases, depth also interacts with your local frost line and drainage. In colder climates, an undersized base lets water collect and freeze underneath, heaving and cracking the surface above it over winter. That's why base-layer depths in this guide sit at 4–6 inches rather than the 2 inches that's plenty for a purely decorative bed — the extra depth is doing structural work, not just filling space.

How Depth Affects Material Quantity

Because volume is area × depth, doubling your depth doubles your material and your cost — the relationship is directly linear, and it's the single biggest lever in any gravel estimate. A 200 sq ft patio at 2 inches needs about 1.23 cubic yards; the same patio at 4 inches needs about 2.47 cubic yards — roughly double the tons, the delivery cost, and (if you're buying bagged) the number of bags. That's why getting depth right before you order matters more than getting your area measurement precise to the inch: a rounding error in area might shift your total by a few percent, but choosing the wrong depth can shift it by 50% or more.

Once you've picked a depth from the table above, plug it into the pea gravel calculator or any other calculator on this site along with your area to get an exact cubic yard, ton, and bag count.

Loose Depth vs. Compacted Depth

The depths in this guide are finished, compacted depths — what the gravel measures once it's settled and, for structural layers, been tamped or rolled down. Freshly spread, uncompacted gravel sits noticeably higher than that, since loose material has more air gaps between stones. As a rule of thumb, budget an extra 10–15% of depth when you first spread a compactable material like crusher run or road base, so that after compaction you land on the target depth rather than falling short of it. Purely decorative materials like pea gravel and river rock aren't compacted the same way, so what you spread is close to what you keep.

Frequently asked questions

How deep should gravel be?

It depends on the use: 2 inches for decorative ground cover, 2–3 inches for walkways and patios, 3–4 inches for drainage beds, and 4–6 inches for driveways and areas with vehicle traffic.

Is 2 inches of gravel enough?

Two inches is enough for decorative ground cover and light foot-traffic paths, but not for driveways or areas that need to bear weight — those need 4 inches or more of compacted base material.

How deep should gravel be under a shed?

Most sheds want 4–6 inches of compacted crushed stone as a base, which levels the ground and keeps moisture from sitting under the floor. Check your specific shed manufacturer's foundation requirements, since some prefill or kit foundations specify their own depth.

Does a deeper gravel layer mean better drainage?

Not necessarily — drainage depends more on the material's gradation than its depth. A deeper layer of dense-graded material like crusher run can actually drain worse than a shallower layer of clean, washed crushed stone. For drainage-focused projects, prioritize an open-graded material like #57 stone over simply adding depth.

Should I spread gravel deeper than the target depth?

For compactable base materials like crusher run or road base, yes — spread it about 10–15% deeper than your target and compact it down, since loose material settles once tamped or rolled. Decorative materials like pea gravel and river rock aren't compacted, so spread those closer to your actual target depth.
