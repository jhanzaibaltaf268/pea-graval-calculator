How Much Gravel Do I Need?

Most projects need between 1 and 10 cubic yards of gravel. To find your number, multiply the area in square feet by the depth in inches and divide by 324, then add 10% for waste. Multiply cubic yards by 1.4 to convert to tons. A 10 x 12 ft patio at 3 inches deep, for example, needs about 1.1 cubic yards, or 1.7 tons once you add the extra. Step-by-step: how to calculate gravel

Step 1: Measure your area

Measure length and width in feet. For a circle, measure the diameter and use area = 3.14 x radius x radius. For an irregular shape, split it into rectangles, circles or triangles, calculate each one, and add them together.

Step 2: Choose your depth

Use 1 to 2 inches for garden beds, 2 inches for paths, 3 to 4 inches for patios and shed bases, and 4 to 6 inches or more for driveways. See the full gravel depth guide for depth by project type.

Step 3: Calculate cubic yards

Multiply area (sq ft) by depth (in inches), then divide by 324. If you already have the depth in feet, divide by 27 instead. Both versions of the formula do the same thing — they just start from different depth units.

Step 4: Convert to tons if needed

Multiply cubic yards by 1.4, or by your supplier's own density figure if they give you one. Quarries typically bill by the ton, so this is the number you'll actually order and pay for.

Worked examples

Example 1: French drain, 1 ft wide x 1 ft deep x 50 ft long

Volume is 50 cubic feet, which is 1.85 cubic yards, or about 2.6 tons. With 10% extra, that's 2.04 cubic yards (2.85 tons). Line the trench with geotextile fabric before filling it.

Example 2: shed base, 8 x 10 ft at 4 inches

80 sq ft x 4 in ÷ 324 = 0.99 cubic yards, or about 1.4 tons. With 10% extra, that's 1.09 cubic yards (1.5 tons). Extend the base 6 to 12 inches beyond the shed footprint on each side.

Example 3: a path 3 ft wide at 2 inches

Each 10 ft of path is 30 sq ft, which needs about 0.19 cubic yards (0.26 tons). Multiply by the length of your path in tens of feet to get the total.

How much extra gravel should I order?

Add 10% for waste and settling. Gravel settles as it's compacted and spreads a little at the edges as it's leveled, so the theoretical minimum from the formula is never quite what you'll use. Add 10% on firm, flat ground, and 15% on soft ground or over a deep base. Round up to the next half cubic yard when you place the order. A second delivery to cover a small shortfall almost always costs more than ordering a few extra shovelfuls up front.

Useful conversion tips

  • 1 cubic yard = 27 cubic feet
  • 1 cubic yard of gravel = about 1.4 tons = about 2,800 lb
  • 1 ton of gravel = about 0.71 cubic yards
  • 1 ton covers about 116 sq ft at 2 inches, 77 sq ft at 3 inches, or 58 sq ft at 4 inches
  • 1 cubic yard fills about 4.5 loads of a 6 cubic foot wheelbarrow

Bulk vs bags

Bags (usually 0.5 cubic foot, around 50 lb each) make sense for projects under about half a cubic yard — small borders, potted plants, or filling in a gap. Above that, bulk delivery is almost always the cheaper option per cubic yard, since you're not paying for packaging or extra handling. As a reference point, one cubic yard is 54 bags of 0.5 cubic foot.

Questions to ask your gravel supplier before you pay

  • What is the density (tons per cubic yard) you bill by?
  • Is the price per ton or per cubic yard, and is delivery included?
  • Is there a minimum order or a delivery fee?
  • Is the stone washed or unwashed, and what is its size grade?
  • How much does your truck hold, and where can it dump on my property?
  • Can you split the load between two spots?

Common ordering mistakes

  • Using loose (fluffed) depth instead of compacted, finished depth.
  • Mixing feet and inches in the formula.
  • Forgetting the compacted base layer under a patio or driveway.
  • Not adding anything for waste.
  • Ordering by volume from one supplier and weight from another without converting between the two.

Frequently asked questions

How much gravel do I need for my project?

Multiply the area in square feet by the depth in inches, then divide by 324 to get cubic yards. Multiply cubic yards by 1.4 to get tons. Add 10% for waste before you order.

Should I order extra gravel?

Yes, about 10% extra on firm ground, or 15% on soft ground or over a deep base, to cover settling, compaction and spillage.

Do I need a base layer under gravel?

For paths, patios, shed bases and driveways, yes: a compacted base prevents sinking and gives a stable surface. Garden beds and tree rings usually don't need one.

How much gravel do I need per square foot?

About 0.17 cubic feet per square foot at a 2-inch depth, or 0.25 cubic feet per square foot at 3 inches. Multiply by your total square footage for a quick estimate.

What if my area isn't a simple rectangle?

Split it into rectangles, circles and triangles, calculate the cubic yards for each shape separately, and add the results together.

How much gravel do I need for a 100 ft driveway?

At 12 ft wide and 4 inches deep, that's about 14.8 cubic yards, or 20.7 tons, before adding the usual 10% for waste.

Method and sources

Volume = area x depth, converted at 27 cubic feet per cubic yard (or 324 when depth is entered in inches rather than feet). Weight = volume x density, using 1.4 tons per cubic yard as a typical figure for gravel and crushed stone. Always confirm the density your own supplier uses, since it varies by material and moisture content.
