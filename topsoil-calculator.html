How to calculate cubic yards of topsoil

Multiply length (ft) by width (ft) by depth (in) and divide by 324. If you know the area in square feet, multiply it by the depth in inches and divide by 324.

cubic yards = square feet × depth (in) ÷ 324

Example: 1,000 sq ft of lawn at 4 inches deep is 1,000 x 4 ÷ 324 = 12.35 cubic yards.

How deep should topsoil be?

For a new lawn, 4 to 6 inches of topsoil over loosened ground is a common recommendation. For a vegetable garden or raised bed, 8 to 12 inches. For top-dressing an existing lawn, 1/4 to 1/2 inch is plenty. Add 5 to 10% to the volume because topsoil compacts as it settles.

Raised bed topsoil calculator

For a raised bed, use the inside length and width and the depth of soil you want. A 4 x 8 ft bed filled 10 inches deep needs 32 x 10 ÷ 324 = 0.99 cubic yards. Many gardeners mix topsoil with compost rather than filling a bed with topsoil alone.

Bulk topsoil or bags?

Bagged topsoil is often sold in 0.75 or 1 cubic foot bags, so one cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, or 27 bags of 1 cubic foot. Bags are practical below about a half yard; above that, bulk delivery is usually cheaper per yard but carries a delivery fee. Prices vary by region, so get quotes from local suppliers.

How much does a cubic yard of topsoil weigh?

Roughly 1 to 1.2 tons (2,000 to 2,400 lb) when moist, and less when dry. The weight changes a lot with moisture and organic content, so the weight shown above is only a planning figure. Most suppliers sell topsoil by the cubic yard, which avoids the problem.
