How much pea gravel do I need?

Multiply the area by the depth, then convert to cubic yards. In imperial units the shortcut is:

cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 324

324 is 27 cubic feet per cubic yard times 12 inches per foot. To get tons, multiply cubic yards by the density — about 1.4 tons per cubic yard for pea gravel. To get a safe order quantity, add 10% for compaction and waste.

How much pea gravel do I need per square foot?

A cubic yard of pea gravel covers 324 square feet at 1 inch, 162 at 2 inches, 108 at 3 inches and 81 at 4 inches. Per square foot at 2 inches deep you need about 0.006 cubic yards, or 0.17 cubic feet.

Worked pea gravel examples

Example 1: patio, 10 × 12 ft at 3 inches

Area is 120 sq ft. Volume is 120 × 3 ÷ 324 = 1.11 cubic yards, or 30 cubic feet. Weight is 1.11 × 1.4 = 1.56 tons. With a 10% allowance, order 1.22 cubic yards or about 1.7 tons — 60 bags of 0.5 cu ft, or 66 with the allowance.

Example 2: garden path, 3 ft wide × 30 ft long at 2 inches

Area is 90 sq ft. Volume is 90 × 2 ÷ 324 = 0.56 cubic yards. Weight is 0.78 tons. With 10% extra, order 0.61 cubic yards or about 0.86 tons — a small enough job that bags may be easier than a bulk delivery.

Example 3: circular bed, 10 ft diameter at 3 inches

Area is 3.14 × 5 × 5 = 78.5 sq ft. Volume is 78.5 × 3 ÷ 324 = 0.73 cubic yards. Weight is 1.02 tons. With 10% extra, order 0.80 cubic yards or about 1.12 tons.

How to use this pea gravel calculator

  1. Pick the shape. Rectangle, circle, or a border/ring if you're filling around a feature such as a tree or fire pit.
  2. Enter the dimensions. Measure in feet and inches or metres and centimetres. For an irregular area, split it into rectangles and circles, calculate each, and add the totals.
  3. Set the depth. Use the depth table below if you're unsure. Depth is the finished, settled layer, not the loose depth when you spread it.
  4. Check density and bag size. Keep the default 1.4 tons per cubic yard unless your supplier gives a different figure, and choose the bag size printed on the label.
  5. Read the results and order. Round up to the next half yard, or to the next whole bag.

What is pea gravel, and what size is it?

Pea gravel is small, smooth, rounded stone, usually about 1/8 to 3/8 inch across, named for its resemblance to peas. It's a natural, water-worn aggregate, typically screened and washed, ranging from tan and cream to grey and brown. Because the stones are round, they drain well but don't lock together the way angular crushed stone does.

How many bags of pea gravel do I need?

Bags are sold by cubic foot or by weight, commonly 0.5 cubic foot (about 50 lb). One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, so you need 54 half-cubic-foot bags per cubic yard, or about 40 bags per ton.

bags needed = cubic feet ÷ bag size (cu ft)

For example, a path that needs 15 cubic feet takes 30 bags of 0.5 cubic foot. Always check the label — bag sizes vary by brand, and some are sold by weight only.

Bulk vs bagged pea gravel: which is cheaper?

Bagged gravel costs more per cubic foot because you pay for packaging, handling and retail margin, but it has no delivery fee and no minimum. Bulk is usually cheaper per ton once you need more than about half a cubic yard, but delivery fees and minimum loads can erase the saving on small jobs.

Calculating circular, curved and irregular areas

  • Circle: area = 3.14 × radius × radius. A 10 ft diameter circle is 78.5 sq ft.
  • Ring or border (filling around an object): area of the outer circle minus area of the inner circle.
  • Triangle: area = base × height ÷ 2.
  • Irregular shape: split it into rectangles and circles, calculate each area, then add them.

Convert the total area to cubic yards with the depth formula above. If you already know the area in square feet, use the Square Feet to Cubic Yards Calculator.

Why you should add 10% extra

Pea gravel settles into the base and spreads at the edges as you rake it, and a bulk load is rarely exactly the quantity you ordered. Adding 10% covers settling and spillage. Add 15% if the base is soft or uneven, and round up to the next half cubic yard. Running short means a second delivery fee and a possible colour mismatch between batches.

What is pea gravel used for?

  • Walkways and garden paths — a comfortable, permeable surface that's easy to lay.
  • Patios and seating areas — a low-cost alternative to pavers when set over a compacted base with edging.
  • Playgrounds — gentle underfoot, but loose fill needs enough depth for your equipment, and it isn't wheelchair-accessible.
  • Drainage and French drains — water passes through quickly; use washed stone and fabric to stop soil from clogging it.
  • Dog runs — drains fast and is easy on paws, though it needs periodic cleaning.
  • Garden beds and tree rings — suppresses weeds and holds a clean edge.
  • Fire pit surrounds — use with care; wet river-style stones can crack under heat, so keep pea gravel away from direct flame and use lava rock or fire-rated media inside the ring.
  • Concrete mix and pipe bedding — smaller grades such as #8 and #89.

Pea gravel pros and cons

Pros: excellent drainage, smooth underfoot, many colours, easy to spread and relocate, and no mixing or curing.

Cons: the stones shift and migrate without edging, it's hard to walk or wheel on, it needs top-ups over time, it holds leaves and debris that need raking, and it's difficult to shovel snow from. If you need a firmer surface, consider crushed stone, or see Types of Gravel and the River Rock Calculator.

Common pea gravel estimating mistakes

  • Using loose depth instead of finished depth. Calculate for the settled layer, then add the 10% allowance.
  • Mixing units. Convert inches to feet or use the 324 formula; don't multiply feet by inches.
  • Ignoring the base layer. A patio or path needs a compacted base under the pea gravel; calculate that separately as crushed stone.
  • Skipping edging and fabric. Without edging the stones spread; without fabric they sink into soil and weeds push through.
  • Using the wrong density. Washed, dry and wet pea gravel weigh differently — ask your supplier for the tons per cubic yard they use.
  • Forgetting access. A full truck can't always reach the site; check the dump location before ordering bulk.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a cubic yard of pea gravel weigh?

About 1.4 tons, or roughly 2,800 lb. Washed, dry pea gravel can run from about 2,600 to 3,000 lb per cubic yard, and wet gravel weighs more.

How many tons of pea gravel are in a cubic yard?

About 1.4 tons at the default density. Multiply cubic yards by 1.4 to get tons, or divide tons by 1.4 to get cubic yards.

How much area does a ton of pea gravel cover?

About 116 square feet at 2 inches deep, 77 square feet at 3 inches, and 58 square feet at 4 inches, assuming 1.4 tons per cubic yard.

How much does 5 cubic feet of pea gravel weigh?

About 520 lb at the default density, which is roughly 10 bags of 0.5 cubic foot.

How much does a 50 lb bag of pea gravel cover?

A 0.5 cubic foot bag covers about 2 square feet at 3 inches deep, or 3 square feet at 2 inches. Use the bag size printed on your product, because brands differ.

What depth of pea gravel should I use?

Use 1 to 2 inches for garden beds, 2 inches for paths, and 2 to 3 inches for patios and dog runs, always over a compacted base. See our gravel depth guide for a full breakdown.

How much does pea gravel cost per ton?

Prices vary by region, colour, whether it's washed, and delivery distance, so get a couple of local quotes. Enter the price per ton, cubic yard or bag in the gravel cost calculator to see your total.

Is pea gravel good for drainage?

Yes — pea gravel's rounded stones leave small gaps that let water pass through easily, making it a common fill around French drains, downspouts, and drain fields. For heavier drainage beds, larger river rock is often preferred.

What should I put down before pea gravel?

Remove the grass or topsoil, level and compact the soil, lay landscape fabric, add 3 to 4 inches of compacted crushed stone for patios and seating areas, then install edging before spreading the pea gravel.

Can I use pea gravel for a driveway?

It's not recommended as the main surface, because rounded stone shifts under vehicles. Build the driveway from angular crushed stone and use the Gravel Driveway Calculator to size the layers.

What is the difference between pea gravel and river rock?

Pea gravel is small (about 1/8 to 3/8 inch), while river rock is larger, usually 1 to 3 inches or more. Both are rounded. Pea gravel suits walking surfaces, and river rock suits decorative beds and dry creek beds. See the River Rock Calculator.
